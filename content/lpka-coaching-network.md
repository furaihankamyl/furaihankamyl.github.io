PKBM Hasanah runs education and training for the children at LPKA Kelas II Jakarta. When we interviewed them, they told us about a problem. A program could run well with one partner, and LPKA still struggled to carry it over to the next. So the quality of coaching changed every time a partner changed. Ruang Damai told us something similar, with gaps in facilities and in coordination between actors at different levels.

Those two interviews shaped the whole study. This is the PKM-RSH project I wrote about in [the PIMNAS story](writing/pimnas/). I did it with Adinda Puspita Ningrum, who was first author, and Muhammad Rafli Gebrena, supervised by Dr. Lina Miftahul Jannah. I led the method. Our question was practical. How should LPKA manage its partners so that a good program survives a change in people, partners, or location?

We were not the first to see the problem. PUSKAPA UI (2023) had already named three issues in LPKA coaching programs. They were not built on proper needs assessments, resources were thin, and actors at different levels did not coordinate well. Muzakki and Subroto (2023) added one more: there were no standard operating procedures.

## Who works with LPKA

We started with what LPKA publishes about itself. We read 260 articles on the websites of LPKA Kelas II Jakarta and the Directorate General of Corrections, and kept the 56 that named actors in coaching programs. From those we coded 80 actors by level, time, and type of support, and mapped how they interact in Gephi 0.10.1. A map built from websites only shows what an institution chooses to publish. So we checked it on the ground, through observation at LPKA on 4 July 2024 and interviews with LPKA and its partners, from Ditjenpas and the police to Bapas Jakarta Selatan, Disdukcapil Jakarta, Ruang Damai, and PKBM Hasanah.

## What the map showed

**The network is strongest at the regional level and weakest at the local level.** Regional actors had 6 connections each on average, with a density of 0.400. Local, community, and private actors had 3.4, with a density of 0.065.

This turned out to matter when LPKA moved. National and regional actors are connected to each other, so information keeps moving even when one of them drops out. Local actors tend to stand alone. When LPKA moved from Salemba to Cinere in 2020, its local partners around Cempaka Putih were replaced by new ones around Cinere. Some programs that had worked well could not be run again.

**The network also got looser after the move.** Density fell from 0.193 in Salemba (2018 to 2020) to 0.097 in Cinere (2020 to May 2024). Many new ties in Cinere stood alone, with no link to old or new partners. When partners do not talk to each other, what one program learned does not reach the next.

The information sharing tab explains PKBM Hasanah's complaint. At the local level, the partner often owns the program's technical framework, not LPKA. PKBM Hasanah designs the education and training curriculum, so PKBM Hasanah decides whether that curriculum gets passed on. **When the framework sits with the partner, the program only lasts as long as the partner stays.**

## What makes a program last

We put the findings into a model with four aspects that keep a coaching program going, and turned each into a step LPKA can take.

<div class="table-wrap"><table><thead><tr><th>Aspect</th><th>Step for LPKA</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Oriented to children's needs</td><td>Survey the children's needs across infrastructure, education, health, religion, personality, and social support, then direct resources there</td></tr><tr><td>A clear technical framework</td><td>Write each program with an SOP, measurable goals, a timeline, resources, and monitoring, so it survives a change of people or partner</td></tr><tr><td>Actors at every level</td><td>Map relevant actors by level and affiliation before involving them in a program</td></tr><tr><td>Continuous evaluation</td><td>Build a standard evaluation scheme with clear indicators that a new partner can take over</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

We also made three recommendations for the levels above LPKA. The regional office of the Ministry of Law and Human Rights should pick programs that fit each LPKA. Ditjenpas guidelines should give regional offices more room to work with local partners. And each program should involve the partners relevant to it.

Website articles only capture what LPKA chose to publish, and we treated each period as a fixed snapshot. A year later, my thesis rebuilt the network from 1,789 Instagram posts and found the same direction of change with far more data. That is in [the thesis explainer](research/lpka-network-governance/).
