**Category:** Research and Analysis | **Year:** 2025

I started running in April 2025 for a reason I am not proud of.

A coworker invited me along, and I said yes. Then I bought the gear that comes with deciding to take something seriously. Running shoes, proper clothes, a Garmin 165. And the moment I paid, a question appeared in my head that had nothing to do with fitness. What is the return on this? **There is exactly one way to get a return on a running watch. You have to run.**

So I ran. Not because I loved it, but because some part of my brain refused to let the purchase go to waste. Somewhere in that process, something shifted. I started feeling alive every time I ran. Free, in a way that is hard to describe properly. Car Free Day became the clearest version of that feeling. Watching Jakarta's streets fill with people running, walking, and just being outside, all at once, was something I looked forward to every week. I also got good at finding freebies near FX Senayan, which is its own small joy. By the time I noticed it, I had fallen in love with running. Not for the watch. Not for the shoes. For the thing itself.

Once Car Free Day was not enough, I started signing up for actual events:

* Crystalin Run Xperience, half marathon
* Belanegarun, 5k
* Borobudur Marathon, 10k
* Capital Market Run, 10k
* BTN Jakarta International Marathon 2026, half marathon

![Race bib at BTN Jakarta International Marathon 2026, ready to conquer Jakarta](images/jakim-racepack.jpg)

What keeps me coming back is not the race pack, although that helps. It is the personal records. **Every event so far has given me a new one**, and that small proof of progress is addictive in the best way. Current personal bests:

**5K: 24:10**
**10K: 51:25**
**Half Marathon: 1:58:15**

No full marathon yet. That is the goal for next year. Let's go.

My gym routine is less consistent than my running, and I will not pretend otherwise. The motivation is simple. It reduces injury risk from running and helps with the stiffness that comes from sitting through long workdays. Not glamorous. Just practical. On weekdays I almost always make time for one or the other. The plan is usually before work. The reality is usually after work, because I am someone who wakes up late more often than I would like to admit. The gym in the evening has become my honest default.

Weekends look different. When I am alone, I usually end up at a cafe in Blok M, working through whatever did not get finished during the week. It is quiet, focused, and exactly what I need when the goal is just to get things done. When I am with friends, it looks completely different. We end up at GI, or at one of the more interesting cafes around the city, sometimes ones built around board games. The agenda is rarely structured. Mostly catching up, talking about how life is going, and occasionally getting unreasonably competitive over a card game.

![Board game session with friends at a cafe](images/board-game-cafe.jpg)

![Catching up with university friends over coffee](images/friends-cafe.jpg)

None of this is separate from the work I do. Running taught me something about consistency that shows up in how I approach long projects. Weekends with friends remind me that not everything needs to be optimized or productive to be worth doing.
