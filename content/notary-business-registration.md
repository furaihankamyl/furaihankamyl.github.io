That step sits outside the risk-based OSS reform.</li><li>Across 190 countries in EoDB 2020, the 102 that require a notary impose about 2 more procedures, 7 more days, and 23 more points of cost as a share of income per capita.</li><li>The gaps in procedures and cost are significant for men and women founders. Indonesia's Online Single Submission system can issue a business identification number, the NIB, in under an hour. By 2023 it had issued 7,146,105 of them. For a limited liability company, a PT, that hour comes last. Before OSS sees the company, a notary has to reserve its name, draw up the deed of establishment, and file it with the Ministry of Law and Human Rights.

The Job Creation Law and PP No. 5 of 2021 simplified licensing into a risk-based system. The notary's role was left out of that reform and is still governed separately, in Permenkumham No. 21 of 2021. We wanted to know two things. Does requiring a notary actually make registering a company harder? And if it does, what should reform aim for?

## Testing the notary across 190 countries

A single country cannot answer the first question, because there is nothing to compare it with. The World Bank's Ease of Doing Business (EoDB) data can. Its 2020 edition, the last one published, records how many procedures, days, and how much cost it takes to start a company in 190 countries.

We coded every country by whether its process requires a notary. 102 countries do. 88 do not, or make it optional. We then compared the two groups on procedures, time, and cost with an independent t-test. EoDB records the process separately for men and women founders, so we ran each test for both. We included all 190 countries instead of sampling, to reduce selection bias in a comparison that could not be randomized.

Countries that dropped the notary requirement include New Zealand, Singapore, and the United Kingdom, ranked 1st, 2nd, and 8th in EoDB. In New Zealand, name checks and company details go through a single standard digital form.

## Two countries that changed it

The second question needed cases. We looked for reforms that started where Indonesia is now, with fragmented rules for setting up companies and a notary in the process. Two fit: the pilot Free Trade Zones and Free Trade Port in China, and Macedonia's 2005 reform. We read both through the IFC and World Bank (2013) framework, which splits registration reform into legal, business process, and institutional changes.

## Two options for Indonesia

The cases translate into two reform objectives. They differ in how far they go with the notary.

<div class="table-wrap"><table><thead><tr><th></th><th>Option 1: integrate the notary</th><th>Option 2: eliminate the notary</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Modeled on</td><td>Macedonia</td><td>China FTZ and FTP, and New Zealand</td></tr><tr><td>Core idea</td><td>Bring the AHU system into OSS so notaries work inside it</td><td>Replace legalization with standard forms and certified digital signatures in OSS</td></tr><tr><td>Legal change</td><td>Harmonize Law 40/2007, Perpu 2/2022, PP 5/2021, and Permenkumham 21/2021</td><td>Revise PP 5/2021 and Permenkumham 21/2021</td></tr><tr><td>OSS becomes</td><td>One stop for name check, name payment, standard legalization form, then NIB and licenses</td><td>The whole process under the Ministry of Investment/BKPM</td></tr><tr><td>Notary role</td><td>Keeps the first three steps</td><td>No longer required</td></tr><tr><td>Main risk</td><td>Cost and time may stay close to today</td><td>Opposition from notaries, and OSS needs capacity to replace the ministry's technical role</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

**Integration is easier to accept. Elimination does more to cut the burden.** The quantitative finding favors elimination, since the burden is tied to the notary requirement itself. Integration keeps the notary's legal expertise and data protection role, but it may leave cost and time close to where they are now.

The study has limits. EoDB was discontinued, so the data ends with observations from 2019, before the Job Creation Law took effect. The comparison shows a difference between groups of countries. It does not isolate the notary as the only cause. The next step is the technical procedure for either option and the advocacy network to support it.

The paper won first place for both paper and presentation at Temu Administrator Muda Indonesia 2024.

OSS is fast by its own measure. The burden sits in the steps a founder takes before reaching it.
