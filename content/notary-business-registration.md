<div class="takeaways"><div class="takeaways-title">Key takeaways</div><ul><li>To set up a PT in Indonesia, you still need a notary. The 2021 licensing reform left that step alone.</li><li>We compared 190 countries. The 102 that require a notary add about 2 procedures, 7 days, and 23 points of cost as a share of income per capita.</li><li>The gaps in procedures and cost hold for men and women founders. The gap in time is borderline.</li><li>China's free trade zones dropped the notary. Macedonia moved registration into one register and went from 48 days to 3.</li><li>Indonesia can bring notaries inside OSS, or replace them with standard forms and certified digital signatures.</li></ul></div>

OSS can issue a business identification number, the NIB, in under an hour. By 2023 it had issued 7,146,105 of them. For a limited liability company, a PT, that hour comes last. Before OSS sees the company, a notary has to reserve its name, draw up the deed of establishment, and file it with the Ministry of Law and Human Rights.

The Job Creation Law and PP No. 5 of 2021 rebuilt licensing around risk. The notary was left out of that reform. It still runs on its own rule, Permenkumham No. 21 of 2021. Adinda and I wanted to know two things. Does the notary actually make starting a company harder? And if it does, what should change?

## 190 countries, one question

One country cannot answer that. There is nothing to compare it with. So we used the World Bank's Ease of Doing Business data. Its 2020 edition, the last one, records the procedures, days, and cost of starting a company in 190 countries.

We sorted every country by one thing. Does it require a notary? 102 do. 88 do not, or make it optional. Then we compared the two groups with an independent t-test. EoDB records men and women founders separately, so we ran every test twice. We used all 190 countries instead of a sample. We could not randomize, so we did not want to choose which countries counted.

## Two countries that changed it

The numbers told us the notary adds weight. They did not tell us what to do about it. So we looked for countries that started where Indonesia is now, with scattered rules and a notary in the middle. We found two. China's pilot free trade zones and free trade port, and Macedonia in 2005. We read both through the IFC and World Bank (2013) framework, which splits reform into legal, business process, and institutional changes.

## Two options for Indonesia

Both cases lead to the same two options. The difference is what happens to the notary.

<div class="table-wrap"><table><thead><tr><th></th><th>Option 1: integrate the notary</th><th>Option 2: eliminate the notary</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Modeled on</td><td>Macedonia</td><td>China FTZ and FTP, and New Zealand</td></tr><tr><td>Core idea</td><td>Bring the AHU system into OSS so notaries work inside it</td><td>Replace legalization with standard forms and certified digital signatures in OSS</td></tr><tr><td>Legal change</td><td>Harmonize Law 40/2007, Perpu 2/2022, PP 5/2021, and Permenkumham 21/2021</td><td>Revise PP 5/2021 and Permenkumham 21/2021</td></tr><tr><td>OSS becomes</td><td>One stop for name check, name payment, standard legalization form, then NIB and licenses</td><td>The whole process under the Ministry of Investment/BKPM</td></tr><tr><td>Notary role</td><td>Keeps the first three steps</td><td>No longer required</td></tr><tr><td>Main risk</td><td>Cost and time may stay close to today</td><td>Opposition from notaries, and OSS needs capacity to replace the ministry's technical role</td></tr></tbody></table></div>

**Integration is easier to sell. Elimination cuts more.** Our numbers favor elimination, since the burden follows the notary requirement itself. Integration keeps the notary's legal expertise and its role in protecting data. It may also leave cost and time close to where they are now.

It was the hardest recommendation to write, because it is straightforwardly bad news for one profession. We learned that, perhaps, no public policy can benefit all parties.

The data has limits. EoDB was discontinued, so it stops at 2019, before the Job Creation Law took effect. We can show a gap between groups of countries. We cannot prove the notary alone causes it. The next step would be the technical procedure for either option, and the people needed to push it.

We presented the paper at Temu Administrator Muda Indonesia 2024, hosted by Universitas Gadjah Mada in Yogyakarta. It won first place for paper and for presentation.

OSS is fast. A founder just reaches it last.
