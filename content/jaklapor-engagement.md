JakLapor won the Champion Project award at the WSIS Prizes 2023. A reporting platform only works if people use it, and the literature gave us two reasons to expect some groups to use it less. Sun (2016) found that women can find it harder to express their views on digital platforms. Studies of Gen Z and Millennials describe different participation styles, even though both grew up online. So we asked whether gender or generation changes young Jakartans' intention to engage with JakLapor.

This paper uses the same August 2023 survey as [our study on public trust](article.html?slug=jaklapor-public-trust), with a wider lens. Trust is one part of engagement here, alongside two others.

## Breaking engagement into parts

We measured public engaging intention (PEI) with the framework of Hu et al. (2019). It has three dimensions, and each dimension has its own indicators. Measuring each part separately matters. A single engagement score could hide a gap in one part behind strength in another.

The survey reached 361 people. We excluded 8 who fell outside the criteria of living in Jakarta, excluding Kepulauan Seribu, and being 15 to 42 years old. That left 353, well above the minimum of 96 set by the Lemeshow formula. We compared men with women and Gen Z with Millennials using the Mann-Whitney U test, on each dimension and on the overall score.

## What we found

**Engagement intention is moderate to high across the board.** Only 1 of 353 respondents had low intention. 218 were moderate and 134 were high. Participation motivation had the largest high share of the three dimensions, at 39.38%.

**Gender made no difference on any dimension.** Men and women had similar intention to engage, similar technology acceptance, similar trust, and similar motivation. This differs from Macaya et al. (2021), who found a gender gap in e-government use. Their sample was in Brazil, mostly men, and about e-government in general rather than one feature.

**Between generations, only trust differed (p = .022).** Gen Z's ratio of moderate to high trust was 2.726 to 1. For Millennials it was 1.682 to 1. The overall engagement score showed no generational gap (p = .744). Gen Z's lower trust did not show up as lower overall intention.

## What this means for JakLapor

Two implications follow. Outreach does not need separate strategies for men and women, since both engage at similar levels. Trust-building should be tailored by generation, with Gen Z as the priority, because trust is the one part of engagement where they fall behind.

Like the trust paper, this study uses a sample that leans toward women (60.91%) and Gen Z (66.57%), and it measures intention at one point in time. Intention to engage is also not the same as actual reports filed.

The gap between Gen Z and Millennials is narrow and specific, and it points to one place to start: trust.
