**Category:** Research and Analysis | **Year:** 2024

Indonesia Emas 2045 is usually discussed in demographic and economic terms. A working-age population at its peak, a target position among the world's largest economies.

What that conversation covers less often is the machinery that has to deliver it. A correctional institution for children. A city reporting channel nobody trusts. A company registration procedure that costs founders more than it should. **Those three are what I spent 2024 looking at.**

Three projects, four accomplishments, and three institutions on the receiving end of the recommendations: the Ministry of Law and Human Rights, the DKI Jakarta Provincial Government, and the Ministry of Investment/BKPM.

## The Children Behind the Walls

**PKM-RSH Research Funding Grantee**
**"Network Governance in Supporting the Subjective Well-being of Juvenile Inmates in LPKA"**

LPKA is Indonesia's Lembaga Pembinaan Khusus Anak, the special correctional institution for children. The question my team started with, together with Adinda and Gebrena, was whether the collaborations run by LPKA Kelas II Jakarta were organized around what the children inside actually needed.

We used social network analysis and a single case study design, drawing on institutional site analysis, interviews, and observation. The institution was trying, and the staff had built real programs. What it lacked were comprehensive standard operating procedures, which meant **its partnerships existed without being directed toward specific outcomes.**

Our recommendation to the Ministry of Law and Human Rights followed from that. Build SOPs for each program, project, and policy, then let those objectives determine which partners get engaged.

This project has its own article.

## When Young People Do Not Trust the Reporting Channel

**PKM-AI Research Incentive Awardee**
**"Comprehensive Analysis of Public Trust Levels Among Young Individuals in the JakLapor Feature"**

JakLapor is a public reporting feature run by the DKI Jakarta Provincial Government. What we kept hearing from young users was a concern about data breach, which is difficult to act on while it stays anecdotal. So we measured it.

We ran comparative tests using Mann-Whitney U and Kruskal-Wallis, and an associative study using Spearman correlation, across a questionnaire segmented by gender, region, generation, and education. **Generation Z held significantly lower trust in the feature than Generation Y.**

Our recommendation was that the provincial government run a public needs survey for JakLapor aimed directly at Generation Z, rather than treating young users as one undifferentiated group.

## The Cost of a Signature

**1st Place for Paper and 1st Place for Presentation, Temu Administrator Muda Indonesia 2024**
**"Simplification of Limited Liability Company Registration Procedures Through Notaries"**

![Award ceremony at Temu AdMI 2024](images/activity-indonesia-emas-2.png)

Founders in Indonesia carry procedural, time, and cost burdens to register a company that founders elsewhere do not. We wanted to know how much of that burden came from the notarization requirement itself.

Using independent t-test analysis across multiple case studies, we compared countries that require a notarization process against countries that do not. **Administrative burden was significantly lower where notarization was not required.**

We recommended to the Ministry of Investment/BKPM that the role of notaries in company registration be integrated into the process or removed from it. It was the hardest recommendation to write, because it is straightforwardly bad news for one profession.
